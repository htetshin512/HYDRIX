01 / THE BASICS

Understanding Drought

01 What is drought?

Drought is a prolonged period of unusually dry conditions, generally associated with below-normal rainfall. It can reduce soil moisture, water supplies and the water available for people, agriculture and ecosystems.

Learn more about drought
02 What are the main causes of drought?

Drought is mainly associated with prolonged below-normal rainfall. High temperatures can increase evaporation and soil moisture loss, making dry conditions more severe. Climate patterns such as El Niño can also influence rainfall in some regions.

Explore the causes
03 What are the different types of drought?

Four commonly described types are meteorological drought, agricultural drought, hydrological drought and socioeconomic drought. They describe shortages affecting rainfall, soil moisture, water resources and the needs of society.

View the four types
04 Can drought happen during the rainy season?

Yes. A rainy season does not guarantee consistent rainfall. Extended dry spells or unusually low precipitation during the normal wet season can contribute to drought conditions.

02 / WATER ACCESS

Water Shortages

05 What is the difference between drought and water scarcity?

Drought refers to prolonged unusually dry conditions, usually connected with a lack of rainfall. Water scarcity occurs when available water cannot adequately meet people's needs. Scarcity can result from drought, high demand, limited infrastructure or poor water management.

Compare the differences
06 Why does Myanmar's Central Dry Zone face water challenges?

Myanmar's Central Dry Zone receives relatively limited and seasonal rainfall. Some communities depend on rain-fed farming, wells, reservoirs and irrigation. Unreliable rainfall and difficulties accessing reliable water sources can increase the risk of shortages.

Explore Myanmar's Dry Zone
07 How can water shortages affect communities?

Water shortages can reduce access to drinking water, make hygiene and sanitation more difficult, damage crops and disrupt livelihoods. Households may also need to spend additional time and money obtaining water for essential activities.

Read about community impacts
08 Does rainfall always solve water scarcity?

Not always. Rainfall can replenish water resources, but scarcity may continue when demand is high, storage is inadequate, infrastructure is limited, or the available water is unsafe to use.

03 / RESPONSIBLE WATER USE

Water Conservation

09 Why is water conservation important?

Water conservation reduces unnecessary consumption and helps protect available supplies. It can lower household costs and support better preparedness during periods when water becomes less accessible.

Explore water conservation
10 What are simple ways to save water at home?

Turn off taps when they are not needed, repair leaking pipes, avoid unnecessarily running water and use water-efficient fixtures where possible. Suitable non-drinking water may also be reused for appropriate tasks when it is safe to do so.

View water-saving methods
11 How can schools reduce water waste?

Schools can regularly inspect taps and pipes for leaks, encourage responsible water habits, install efficient fixtures where practical and educate students about water conservation. Staff and students can also report leaks when they notice them.

12 Can rainwater harvesting help conserve water?

Yes. Collecting and storing rainwater during wet periods can provide an additional water source for suitable uses. Proper collection, storage and treatment are important, especially if the water is intended for drinking.

04 / DISASTER RISK REDUCTION

Drought Preparedness

13 How can families prepare before a drought?

Families can reduce unnecessary water use, inspect storage containers, identify reliable water sources and plan how to meet essential drinking, cooking and hygiene needs if regular supplies become limited. Following local weather updates is also important.

Explore preparedness steps
14 What are the early warning signs of drought?

Possible warning signs include prolonged rainfall shortages, unusually dry soil, declining river or reservoir levels and official drought alerts. Monitoring local weather and water information can help communities respond early.

View drought warning signs
15 How should drinking water be stored safely?

Store safe drinking water in clean, food-safe containers with secure covers. Keep them away from contamination and avoid touching stored water with dirty hands or utensils. Follow local public health guidance on water treatment and safe storage.

16 What can communities do to become more drought-resilient?

Communities can encourage water conservation, improve water storage and management, maintain local infrastructure, share reliable information and develop preparedness plans before shortages occur. Planning should consider the needs of vulnerable households and essential services.

Explore community preparedness
